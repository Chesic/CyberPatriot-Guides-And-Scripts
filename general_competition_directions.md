# General Competition Information
You are competing against other schools around the United States 
to secure the respective system that have been given to you. 
These systems can be:
- Linux Mint
- Linux Ubuntu
- Windows Server 2016
- Windows Server 2019
- Windows

Other operating systems can appear in later rounds but are often exclusive to platinum teams only. 
Before hardening these systems you MUST COMPLETE THE FORENSIC QUESTIONS. 
If you don't complete the forensic questions first making changes to the system can make 
it more difficult for you to solve them later. Potentially losing lots of points!
Afterwards you may than start hardening the system. But I would recommend closely 
reading the report given to you for your specific system. 

Other things that can be given to you are:
- Packet Tracers
- Tests/Quizzes
These are very important and can be the difference maker in a competition. 
For tests/quizzes I recommend referencing the course given to you on NetaCad prior 
to the competition.

# Behavior 
1. You can not be inexperienced and resilient to learn, this is a recipe for disaster, and will 
practically result in your team being a man down the entire competition.
2. You should show up to all practices and competitions. This helps team synergy and will prepare
you for the competition if you work on your specific system. 
3. Preparation outside of practices and competitions WILL make your team succeed more when it is 
time to compete.  

# Teamwork
Team sizes may vary but are generally made up of 6 people. So, I am going to
base these directions around that. A team made up of roles doing their best to 
complete their respective jobs is going to be much more efficient than a team where 
everyone is working collectively on random tasks. 
Here is how I would optimize the teams based on abilities:
1. Team members who have the strongest abilities, most of the time Seniors
and Juniors. Should be using their abilities to the fullest. If you have 3 upperclassmen
2 of them should be working on their own individual systems, and the other should be doing
the packet tracer or test/quizzes. It is important that the team member working on the 
packet tracer or test should spend the least amount of time required on their task WITHOUT
losing or missing out on the gain of any points. After this person finishes, they should 
help the team member that is struggling to gain points. 
2.  Team members who have weaker abilities, often freshmen or sophomores. Should be working
on, or helping with what the upperclassmen aren't specialized in. Weather that is a system
that the upperclassmen don't know or simply doing tasks that don't matter quite as much.
A more experienced underclassmen that might be specialized in a specific task, but isn't 
quite as good at is as the upperclassmen should spend time working on that specific system
with an upperclassmen in order to learn and better their skills. 
3. If your team happens to have less upperclassmen or less people who specialize in securing
specific systems, you should have your most experienced team member overlook everyone else. 
Orchestrating what is going on to help the team work towards securing their systems and 
gaining points. To make this easier you can give your underclassmen checklists or references. 
I have listed these in # Other System Hardening References. 
4. Experience is going to supersede someone who doesn't have experience in Cyber Patriot but 
has specialization in a specific operating system. These more experienced team members should
work on helping out inexperienced team members as much as they can before the competition starts. 
Understanding the competition trumps knowing a lot about a specific operating system because you 
can manage your time better and know what does and doesn't give you points. 

I have created two team comps, a less specialized team and a team that is balanced and has
people who are more specialized.

# Team 1 - Specialized Team
Senior 1 - Specializes in Windows Server
Senior 2 - Specializes in Linux
Junior 1 - Specializes in Windows 
Junior 2 - No Specialization 
Sophomore 1 - Specializes in Linux
Freshman 1 - No Specialization

For this team  Comp I would recommend the seniors work on their respective systems as well as the 
specified junior on theirs. The junior that does not have a specialization should be working on 
the quiz/test or packet tracer with the freshmen, after they finish this task they should immediately
help or monitor the others, making sure everything is organized and flowing properly.

# Team 2 - Less Specialized Team
Senior 1 - Unspecialized but has lots of general knowledge
Junior 1 - Specializes in Windows and Windows Server
Junior 2 - No Specialization
Sophomore 1 - Specializes in Linux 
Sophomore 2- No Specialization
Freshmen 1 - No Specialization

With a team configuration like this it is going to be much more difficult to move forward if you don't 
do your best to divide into roles. These roles are not going to be as clear cut as they did with the  
Specialized team. But this team can still compete. Senior 1 should complete the packet tracer or test and 
move on later to helping out people who are not specialized in a system and walking them through basic 
security policies that will help your team gain points. Junior 1 and sophomore 1 should be working
as hard as they can on their systems of course this is what they specialize in. Sophomore 2 and freshmen 1
should be doing their best to learn systems and practices to better specialize themselves in a specific 
operating system. 

Of course, both of these team can be configured differently but this is how I would do it. 

# The use of AI 
1. AI is not always correct, and can hallucinate and produce incorrect commands or answers that 
will lose you points. 
2. Don't run a command without looking over the code that it has given to you. 
3. Relying entirely on AI can result in lazy or incorrect practices that will not help you in the field. 


# Preparation
1. Learning your system and how to navigate it.
2. Learning what gives you points and what doesn't give you points
3. Knowing what will lose you points.
4. Team roles and jobs.
5. Automation

# During the Competition

Systems:
1. As the competition suggests you should complete your forensic questions first. But if you are unable to do so, 
or they are taking a long time to complete. Secure the system without interfering with the forensic questions. 
2. Once you have completed the forensic questions. Begin securing the system how the 'company' has asked you to 
inside of their briefing they have given to you.
3. Move on to more advanced hardening after you have completed tasks 1 and 2.  

Quizzes/Packet Tracer:
1. These should be finished at a pace that maximizes points while allowing you to have to after completion to 
help teammates with other tasks. 

General: 
1. Time management should be prioritized very highly during this competition. If a task is taking to long move 
on to another if you are able to do so. Spending to much time on an individual task can lose you time to complete 
others.
